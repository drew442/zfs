# QAT 512K async cap sweep

Date: 2026-05-27

## Purpose

The eligibility-counter pass showed that `throughput/512K` makes `512K`
records QAT-eligible, but async-cap fallback still keeps QAT byte share around
half of total input. This sweep tested whether raising the fixed async cap from
the current effective `96` improves useful outcomes on both HDD and NVMe.

The test intentionally included `1M` records as a guardrail. Under a
`throughput/512K` profile, `1M` remains above the profile max and is expected to
fall back to software.

## Test Setup

zfs_qat_dc_profile=throughput

zfs_qat_dc_shape_stats=profile

zfs_qat_dc_async_cap_policy=fixed

Benchmark matrix:

- Media: `test-hdd-pool`, `nvme_scratch`
- Record sizes: `512K`, `1M`

- Modes: `qat`, `sw`
- Iterations: `2`

## Summary

Positive speed means QAT was faster than software for that row. CPU delta is
QAT minus software, so negative values mean QAT saved CPU.

| cap | jobs | media | record | speed vs SW | CPU s/GiB delta | QAT byte share | cap skips |
| ---: | ---: | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 96 | 4 | HDD | 512K | +7.8% | -5.26 | 55.8% | 646 |
| 96 | 4 | NVMe | 512K | +2.1% | -4.63 | 45.3% | 798 |
| 96 | 8 | HDD | 512K | +7.1% | -5.66 | 60.0% | 1174 |
| 96 | 8 | NVMe | 512K | +1.6% | -5.95 | 54.3% | 1342 |
| 192 | 4 | HDD | 512K | +9.7% | -5.65 | 54.2% | 668 |
| 192 | 4 | NVMe | 512K | +3.9% | -5.06 | 52.8% | 688 |
| 192 | 8 | HDD | 512K | +0.7% | -6.62 | 58.1% | 1229 |
| 192 | 8 | NVMe | 512K | -1.9% | -6.55 | 60.6% | 1160 |
| 384 | 4 | HDD | 512K | +9.6% | -6.36 | 65.0% | 510 |
| 384 | 4 | NVMe | 512K | -6.3% | -6.48 | 72.8% | 398 |
| 384 | 8 | HDD | 512K | -0.4% | -7.96 | 78.6% | 633 |
| 384 | 8 | NVMe | 512K | -10.9% | -7.25 | 76.0% | 706 |

The `1M` rows had `0%` QAT byte share under this profile because
`throughput/512K` caps the profile max at `512K`. Their small speed differences
are run-to-run software noise and should not be interpreted as QAT effects.

## Interpretation

Raising the cap increases QAT byte share and CPU savings, but it does not
produce a general throughput win:

- Cap `96` is the most balanced setting. It wins on both media and both tested
  job counts while still saving about `4.63` to `5.95` CPU seconds per GiB.
- Cap `192` improves the jobs=4 rows, but it regresses NVMe at jobs=8 and gives
  only a marginal HDD jobs=8 win.
- Cap `384` pushes QAT share to `65%` to `78.6%`, but it materially regresses
  NVMe throughput and does not improve HDD jobs=8.

The current cap policy should not be changed to use all DC instances for
`512K` throughput by default. The evidence supports keeping the current
balanced cap for profile mode and treating higher caps as offload/CPU-saving
bias only, not as a general throughput default.

## Next Target

The remaining useful work is not simply "raise the cap". Better next targets
are:

- Add explicit profile documentation for the cap tradeoff:
  `throughput` optimizes elapsed time, while `offload` may spend throughput to
  save more CPU by allowing higher QAT share.
- Consider a media-bias profile later. HDD and NVMe do not respond identically
  to higher QAT share, and this test reinforces the earlier rotational/flash
  note.
- Continue request-overhead work inside the QAT path; higher caps expose more
  QAT wait and setup cost rather than removing it.
